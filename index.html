Internet measurement at Green Face Labs

Published 2026-10-07 · green-face-labs.github.io

Green Face Labs studies how AI agents appear on the internet and how they behave. Part of that work is measurement: we read public files that websites publish for AI agents, and we operate research sensors that record what reaches them. This page explains what we do, what we never do, how to recognize our traffic, and how to opt out. Our research protocol governs this work and is available on request.

Our census

How to recognize it

Every request identifies itself:

User-Agent: GreenFaceLabsCensus/0.1 (+https://green-face-labs.github.io)

and comes only from these addresses: 147.15.52.72 and 45.77.141.165. A machine-readable list is available at census-ips.txt.

What it reads

For each website on a public research ranking (the Tranco list), the census requests up to four public files, once per measurement run:

FileWhat it is
/.well-known/agent-card.json, /.well-known/agent.jsonAgent cards, which describe an AI agent and how to reach it
/.well-known/ai-plugin.jsonAn AI plugin manifest
/llms.txtA site description written for language models

What it never does

It does not log in, submit forms, follow links, send query strings, test passwords or probe for vulnerabilities. It does not interact with AI agents or servers it finds; it only reads files that site operators chose to publish.

How it behaves

  • At most four requests per site per run, with a short timeout and a size limit.
  • If your site answers 429 Too Many Requests or 503, we stop for that run.
  • We honor robots.txt rules that name GreenFaceLabsCensus. To exclude your site, add:
User-agent: GreenFaceLabsCensus
Disallow: /

Our research sensors

We operate research sensors: servers we own that imitate services used by AI agents, such as agent tool servers and AI developer machines. They hold no real data and serve no real users. They record what reaches them, so we can measure how often automated AI agents — not only conventional scanners — probe this kind of infrastructure.

Our sensors never contact anyone, never send harmful content, and never fetch, open or execute anything they receive. Some contain decoy values. Decoys only ever ask a visiting agent to do something harmless and confined to our own systems, such as repeating a code; they never ask for its instructions, its user's data, or any action elsewhere.

For security reasons we do not publish where our sensors are.

What we keep, and for how long

DataHow long
Full IP addresses seen by our sensors7 days, then replaced by a protected code and network prefix
Requests received by our sensors90 days, with passwords and personal data removed on arrival; then only fingerprints and metadata
Agent cards and AI descriptors collected by the census24 months; then archived without any personal fields
Published statisticsIndefinitely, only in aggregate

We publish only aggregate results. We never publish individual IP addresses, sensor locations or decoy values. Sites that publish agent cards for public discovery appear in our reports only in aggregate unless the operator agrees.

Opting out and your rights

E-mail labs@greenface.ai with the domains or IP ranges you want excluded, or with a request to access or delete data about your addresses. We act within 7 days and confirm by e-mail. You do not need to give a reason. We keep the exclusion list so we can continue to honor it.

Our legal basis is legitimate interest in security research (Brazil's LGPD, art. 7, IX; the EU GDPR, art. 6(1)(f)), assessed and documented before collection.

Responsible disclosure

If our measurement shows that an organization exposes something that could harm it, we tell the organization and, where appropriate, its national CERT before anything is published. We never publish details that could be used to attack anyone.

Why we do this

AI agents are becoming actors on the internet. Defenders need an independent, reproducible picture of where agents are and how they behave, published with method and limitations.
